# Personal data

Spec 0007b. A signed-in person answers two consent questions on `Min side`, downloads a JSON file of everything held about them and can have their data erased. The link in the alumni e-mail answers the same two questions without signing in. An administrator merges two records of one person and can erase anyone on their behalf. A nightly retention job anonymises or deletes what has reached the end of its period and audits counts only. The privacy notice on the contact form is generated from the same declarations (`docs/system/administration.md` holds the retention schedule and the record of processing). Only the person answers a consent question for themself or downloads their own file: an administrator's grant does not reach those two commands (R3 item 27).

## Sub-features

- `consent` `/min-side` shows two questions as groups named `Behold opplysningene mine` and `Nyheter for tidligere medlemmer`, each with `Ja` and `Nei`. One click answers one question and leaves the other; the text under the group says `Svaret ditt nå: ikke besvart`, `Ja` or `Nei`. A `Nei` is a record too (it was asked, with which text version).
- `export` `Last ned opplysningene mine` posts to `/api/eksport` and the browser saves `vektorprogrammet-mine-data-<personId>.json`: the person's own rows by table (the `person` row includes `last_admin_place_end`, the end of a place that retention has deleted, when there is one), no other person's name, no credential hash. Each export writes one `person.exported` audit row. Without a session the route answers `403` and `Du har ikke tilgang til dette.`
- `erase-own` `Jeg vil slette opplysningene mine` opens a disclosure; `Slett opplysningene mine` erases and leads to `/slettet` (`Opplysningene dine er slettet`). The last Active administrator is refused: `Den siste administratoren med aktiv konto kan ikke slettes. …`. **No committed spec drives this path:** the spec erases through the panel (below). Drive it by hand, last.
- `erase-admin` on `/kontrollpanel/personer` an administrator opens `Slett opplysningene til <name>` and confirms with `Slett opplysningene` (the way a person with no account is erased). The spec's merge test drives it.
- `alumni-link` the mail to a person whose last engagement ended contains `/alumni?kode=c_<43 characters>`. Without signing in, `Takk for innsatsen i Vektorprogrammet` offers the same two questions; the same link answers again (the first answer stays on record as given); a code that was never minted answers nothing and shows an alert; and the code opens nothing else (`POST /api/eksport` with it is `403`).
- `merge` on `/kontrollpanel/personer` `Slå sammen personer` opens a sheet with `Personen som blir stående` and `Personen som slås sammen inn i den`; `Slå sammen` merges. The list shows one person afterwards; the other record is a tombstone (`anonymisert-<id>@vektorprogrammet.invalid`, `merged_into` the survivor, no name, no `last_admin_place_end`). The survivor holds the later of the two `last_admin_place_end` dates (spec 0026).
- `retention-cron` the Worker's `scheduled` handler with the retention cron (`RETENTION_CRON`, `17 2 * * *`) anonymises a person whose period ended and writes one `retention.ran` audit row whose `counts` are integers per table and name no one (this stack is a local deployment, which applies the run; production only counts while a period is a proposal and writes `retention.reported`). Before it deletes an admin place (`admin_grant`, three years after the place ends: a proposal, so only a local or Preview run does it) it writes the greatest end of the due places into the person's `last_admin_place_end` (never lowering a later date the person holds, and never writing one to an anonymised person); the person's period still counts from that date. Anonymising the person sets the date null. Read it with `select last_admin_place_end from person where id = …` (spec 0026).
- `contact-notice` `/kontakt` shows a `Personvern` region, generated from the declarations: one entry for the four personal fields, `Lagres: <the declared period>`, a `Utkast:` note that names what awaits the operator (the text, the legal bases, the periods) and only that, and no note when nothing does, and recipients that keep their own copy.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- Sign in at `/logg-inn`, then `/min-side`: the two questions, `Last ned opplysningene mine`, and (under a disclosure) the erasure.
- Open the link of an alumni e-mail (`/alumni?kode=…`) in a browser with no session.
- Staff: `Personer` (`/kontrollpanel/personer`) → `Slå sammen personer`, `Slett opplysningene til <name>`.
- Open `/kontakt` and read the notice under the form.
- The cron has no page: `GET <origin>/cdn-cgi/handler/scheduled` runs the tick, and with `?cron=17%202%20*%20*%20*` the retention job (the local trigger of `vp dev`).

## Gotchas

- The spec writes by fixed names and fixed seeded people (Nora's and Ada's consents, Eva's answers, `Dobbel Person`, `Gammel Gunnar`), and the alumni test expects the one mail that the launch's tick queued for Eva. A second run of the spec on one instance fails for that reason: relaunch, do not edit the spec.
- `POST /api/eksport` from a script needs an `Origin` header: the CSRF middleware (`apps/web/src/start.ts`) answers `403` to a POST without one. A form post from the page carries it.
- The panel list hides anonymised people, so a merged or erased record disappears from `Personer`; read the tombstone with the probe, not the page.
- The merge moves what the second record held: memberships, an account, consents (the newest decision wins), a place on the admin list. It refuses with a typed conflict (and no change) for overlapping memberships in one team, two placements in one semester, two accounts, or a place that the person would then grant themself; the browser spec drives the success path only (`packages/db/test/personalDataMerge.postgres.test.ts` has the refusals).
- The photo consent has no page in this slice: `packages/db/test/personalDataPhoto.postgres.test.ts` covers it, the browser does not.
- The retention job and the alumni tick are two crons: the plain `/cdn-cgi/handler/scheduled` runs the tick (it asks people whose engagements ended, and queues mail), only the retention cron string runs the retention job.
- This stack is a `local` deployment, so the retention job changes rows (`retention.ran`). A production deployment only counts while a period is a proposal and audits `retention.reported`; the stack cannot be one, so that side is shown by `apps/web/test/retentionJob.postgres.test.ts` (the Worker's job in each deployment, on a disposable database) and `packages/db/test/retentionGate.postgres.test.ts`, not by a drive.
- When a row is due is computed by the domain (spec 0021: `packages/domain/src/personalData/retentionEnd.ts`); the adapter reads facts in `packages/db/src/personalData/activity.ts` and compares in `sql.ts`. The drive's `Gammel Gunnar` is years past his threshold, so the drive cannot show an error at a boundary (an hour, a change of the clock, a month's end). The gate for a change to those files is `packages/domain/test/personalData/retentionEnd.test.ts` and `packages/db/test/retentionOracle.postgres.test.ts`: the domain against the pre-0021 PostgreSQL arithmetic, re-created from migration `20261027111351_person_last_activity_end`. To see a boundary on the stack, insert people before the retention cron whose last activity (creation only, an admin place's `until`, a membership's end semester) lies about two hours either side of the one-year threshold, and of the five-year threshold (a current against a withdrawn `keepDetails`), and insert `captured_mail` rows whose `sent_at` is 30 days old ± 30 and ± 90 minutes across a change of the clock. Then compare `person.anonymised_at` and the surviving rows with the old arithmetic evaluated inline: `((x at time zone 'Europe/Oslo') + make_interval(…)) at time zone 'Europe/Oslo' <= now()`.
